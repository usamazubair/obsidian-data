#knowledge_build_brain

Here's the adjusted plan for your situation:

**Setup (one time, this week)**

- Install **Anki** on your phone and laptop.
- Create a notes space (Notion, Obsidian, or a GitHub repo) with one page per topic.
- Look at 5–10 job descriptions you're applying to and list the topics that keep repeating.
- Pick a fixed daily study slot (e.g. 30 min early morning or after family time).

**Daily (15–40 minutes)**

- Do your Anki reviews (10–15 min), during a commute, tea break, or before sleep.
- On study days, pick one topic and write a 1-page note **from memory first**, then fill the gaps.
- Add 5–10 flashcards from that note, in your own words.
- Answer 1–2 questions on the topic **out loud**, as if in an interview.

**At work (5 minutes when something comes up)**

- When you solve something interesting (a bug, performance issue, caching, DB query), write a short note: what the problem was, what you did, and why it worked.
- Turn it into 1–2 flashcards and an interview story.

**Weekly**

- One longer session (1–2 hours) on the weekend for a harder topic or system design.
- One mock interview (with a friend, Pramp, or me).
- One full day off, with no study at all.

**Before interviews (a 3–6 week push)**

- Go through your topic list, 1–2 topics per session.
- Prepare 5–6 strong work stories (a hard bug, a performance win, a project you led, a mistake you learned from).
- Revise your own 1-page notes, not tutorials.


**Pace: slow and steady**

- Cover just **1–2 topics per week** from the checklist, not per day. Over 4–6 months you'll cover everything without stress.
- Daily time: **15–20 minutes** of Anki reviews, most days. That's the heart of the system.
- One **30–60 minute** session per week to write a new note and make its flashcards.

**Capture everything in one place**

- Anything you learn, read, or solve at work goes into your notes system the same day, even just 3–4 lines.
- Use the note template (what, why, example, where I used it, interview questions).
- Keep one inbox page for quick capture; organize it into proper notes weekly.

**Keep it in your brain**

- Every note gets a few flashcards. Anki decides when you review, so old topics come back before you forget them.
- Once a month, pick 3–4 random questions and answer them out loud, as a mini self-interview.

**Stay sharp without applying**

- A mock interview every 1–2 months keeps your explaining skill alive.
- Keep your 5–6 work stories updated as you do new work (new projects, bugs, wins).
- Update your CV every few months while achievements are fresh.

**Track progress**

- Tick topics off the checklist as each note and its flashcards are done.
- Revisit and improve old notes when you use that concept again at work.
